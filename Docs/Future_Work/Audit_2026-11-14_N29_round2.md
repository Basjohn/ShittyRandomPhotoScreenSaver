# Audit, 14 Nov 2026: N2.9 round 2 (merged as 9181aafc, ABI 10)

Scope: what round 2 changed, `git diff f030503e 9181aafc^2` (`git log f030503e..9181aafc^2`, eleven commits; the
merged-in R-211 shader commits and round 1's audit document are out of scope). Round 1 is audited in
`Audit_2026-11-14_N29.md`; its finding numbers are cited as "R1 M1" and so on. Read-only on product code. No build, no
load, no window on a real screen. The cited files are unchanged between `9181aafc^2` and `c24e2719`, so the line numbers
hold on main.

The built module (`release/native_module`, built at 9a56407f; there are no `native/core/src` or `shaders` changes
between 9a56407f and `9181aafc^2`) was copied into the worktree. The focused files ran against it:
`test_native_visualizer_route`, `test_native_core_spectrum`, `test_native_mode_content`, `test_native_core_mode`,
`test_visualizer_owner_render_parity`: **67 passed**, none skipped. One probe was written for this audit, on the route
test's own stand-ins, and deleted afterwards. Its output is quoted under M1 and M2.

Counts: **0 high, 6 medium, 8 low.**

## Verdict on the parts asked about

- **The shared refactor (Spectrum on `StreamSource`/`ContentLatch`).** Sound. No lifetime, threading or retire-order
  change reaches a path the harness skips.
  - The bind, unbind, retire, `waitPaused`, `resume` and `releaseRetired` bodies moved into `stream_source.cpp`
    unchanged.
  - The admission rule and the drop edge are byte-for-byte Spectrum's old `sync`.
  - `sync` now takes the presentation before the retire job runs (it ran after). Both run on the render thread with
    the GUI thread blocked, so the order cannot be observed.
  - Destruction:
    - `~SpectrumSource` and `~ModeSource` run the retire job first.
    - `~StreamSource` then frees what is left: the retired readers, then the current one.
    - Derived members (the program, the runtime schema) now die before the readers. That is harmless: the reader holds
      its own `QSharedPointer` to its schema (`PacketReaderConfig::runtimeSchema`, `packet_reader.h:107`).
  - Lock order is `m_mutex` then `m_reportMutex` (in `bind` only). They are never taken the other way round.
- **Teardown against the destruction barrier.** `engine/runtime_destruction.py` watches the generation's QObjects and
  Python owners. None of the native objects are registered with it, and round 2 adds no Python owner.
  - The one new QObject is the composite's `QPropertyAnimation`. It is a child of the item, deleted with it, and its
    `finished` lambda is bound to the item's lifetime.
  - The case of an item deleted during a pending retire is unchanged:
    - `unbindSpectrum` moves the reader to `m_retiring`.
    - The node, which still holds the source, runs `releaseRetired` in its destructor on the render thread at Qt's
      next sync. A node that never existed leaves the item as the last owner, and no read can be in progress.
    - The L8 fix is real: `composite_spectrum_wait_paused` now holds a `shared_ptr`. The waiting harness thread can
      then become the last owner, and nothing reads there.
- **The latch.** The core's `ContentLatch` never hides a card. It keeps the target, which is the problem in M4. The
  route's own latch can still hide the Visualizer for a whole activation, through the take/admission path (M1). The
  route's fade-out is a real fade: Qt animates the item's opacity, and the material honours `state.opacity()`
  (`composite.cpp:228`). It fades back in after a recovery only when the card had been placed before (M2). The unbind
  fade fades an already-cleared target (L3).
- **The core members table.** There are two copies:
  - `kModeCoreMembers` in `mode_content.cpp:26-42`;
  - `MODE_CORE_MEMBERS` in `rendering/native/mode.py:37-53`.

  Drift fails two tests: `test_the_cores_conventions_table_is_the_faces`, which parses the source and has a negative
  control, and `test_the_cores_conventions_are_the_faces`, which compares against the module. The values are a third
  list (L6).
  - The members themselves pass the card through. The rows `u_item_from_frag_*` and `u_window_from_target_*` come
    from `spectrum_presentation`, which is N2.5's rule (a) arithmetic, proved in four rotations at fractional scales.
    So they are right wherever Spectrum's are.
  - `u_dpr` is `presentation.dpr`, as in the Python renderers.
  - The product shape's other input, `v_uv`, is not right: M6.
- **The framebuffer read.**
  - Which thread: the render thread (`updatePaintNode`, `composite.cpp:775-782`) caches it under the stats mutex, and
    the GUI thread reads the cache.
  - Before the first frame: the GUI thread falls back to querying Qt's swapchain itself (`composite.cpp:630-639`). In
    the product that fallback is the common path, not a startup corner (L1).
  - The frame with no target yet: the route raises `WindowNotReady`. Unlike Qt's own `update()`, the request is not
    kept, and nothing re-runs the route when the framebuffer appears. That is a blank only while no new revision
    arrives. When the first frame finally comes, it pops in (M2, M3).
  - The harness hides this. It renders its window once before the owner exists (`owner_render_parity.py:364`), so
    rule (a) never runs the product's unprimed path.
- **"A new activation's first frame shows at once."** This is in the product (`native_route.py:303-310`), not only in
  the tool. It is not a regression: round 1 showed the item at once too, even before a frame. It honours "fade, never
  vanish" only when the first published frame lands at the start of the activation's own fade. That holds for an
  ordinary activation, because the composite inherits the QML scene fade and the card carries `content_fade`. It does
  not hold for a first frame that comes late (M2).
- **Tests.**
  - No test pins a preset name, a preset value or UI wording.
  - Several new tests lack a negative control, or pin product shader or source text (L7).
  - None covers M1 to M6.

## Medium

### M1. A one-off failure of the take or the admission still hides the Visualizer for the whole activation (R1 M1, on another path)

`rendering/quick/visualizer/native_route.py:264-269` (`admit_at_sync` and `take_for_render`, outside the frame `try`
at `:283-302`), `:213-214` (any other exception is the "setup" stage), `:204-207` (a setup failure with the same inputs
returns before `_sync`). The docstring at `:186-189` says a failure of the frame's "take" is retried at the next new
snapshot. The code latches it.

Probe (the route test's stand-ins, 2560x1440 at 150 %, `take_for_render` raising once, then five new snapshots):

```
PROBE B first: True | take raised once -> False stage: setup | five later new snapshots published: [False, False, False, False, False] takes: 2 still failed: True
```

**Failure scenario.** `take_for_render` or `admit_at_sync` raises once while the card is static (a screensaver's
steady state). Examples: a presentation rebase refusal, or a bridge error during an engine handover. The Visualizer
fades out and is never tried again: later snapshots are not even taken, until the identity, the window or the
placement changes. In a session that is usually for good, the class R1 M1 closed for the settings path.

**Suggested fix.** Move `admit_at_sync` and `take_for_render` inside the frame `try`, or classify every exception
raised after `bind` as the frame stage. Keep "setup" for `bind`, `program` and `composite_geometry` only. Correct the
docstring. Test: a bridge whose take raises once, then a new snapshot publishes and fades back in. The control is the
current code, which takes nothing more.

### M2. A card that was never shown pops in at full opacity when its first frame comes late

`native_route.py:303-310` (the fade is `FAILURE_FADE_MS` only when `_faded_out`), `:342-346` (`_faded_out` is set only
by `_hide`, and `_hide` acts only when `_placed` is set), `:234-248` (`_fail` calls `_hide`).

Probe (a refused bind on the first sync, then the card one pixel to the right, so the setup is retried):

```
PROBE A first sync: False ... | after recovery: True | place configs: [{'visible': True, 'fade_ms': 0}]
```

**Failure scenario.** A new activation's first frame shows with `fade_ms = 0`. The design relies on that frame
falling at the start of the activation's own fade (the inherited QML scene fade, the card's `content_fade`). Two paths
deliver the first frame late:

- A setup failure before the first placement, for example a mapping or bind refusal, or a pack that did not load. It
  is retried only when an input changes, typically after the activation fade has ended.
- `WindowNotReady` at activation, followed by an update request only after the fade (M3).

Either way the card appears at full opacity in one frame. That breaks "fade, never vanish" (appearing counts).

**Suggested fix.** Fade in whenever the first frame is not the activation's first opportunity. The simplest form: set
`_faded_out = True` in `_fail` for every failure, placed or not (a `WindowNotReady` at startup then fades in over the
scene fade, which is harmless). A finer form: fade unless the snapshot's `scene_fade * content_fade` is still near 0.
Test: a refused first bind, then recovery, gives `place(..., fade_ms=FAILURE_FADE_MS)`. The control is the probe
above.

### M3. The framebuffer read has no change edge: a lost update is not redone, and a resize is carded with the last sync's size

`composite.cpp:630-639` (`windowPixels`: the last sync's value, else a GUI-thread read), `:775-782` (cached only at the
composite's own sync), `rendering/native/spectrum.py:138-150` (`WindowNotReady`), `native_route.py:199-202`. The
Python item's `update()` is Qt's: it is kept and served at the window's next sync. The route's `update()` is
immediate and is dropped when the window has no framebuffer.

**Failure scenarios.**

1. The window has no swapchain at activation, for example a Visualizer admitted before the window's first expose. The
   update is dropped. The next one comes only with the next published revision (`request_visualizer_present` is
   driven by new revisions). With a paused or idle source, which publishes nothing at rest, the card stays undrawn.
   When a revision finally comes, it pops in (M2).
2. A resize, display move or scale change. The presentation change calls `update()`, and the route cards the frame
   with the new logical size but the previous sync's framebuffer. The H1 ratio is then wrong: the card is misplaced,
   or `composite_geometry` refuses it, which M1's setup latch then holds. Nothing re-cards the frame after the
   composite's next sync stores the new size, so at rest it stays wrong.

The parity tool primes its window (`owner_render_parity.py:364`) precisely to avoid case 1, so rule (a) never runs it.

**Suggested fix.** Give the framebuffer an edge. Options: the composite emits a queued signal from its sync when
`windowPixels` first becomes valid or changes; or the route connects once to the window's `sceneGraphInitialized` and
`afterSynchronizing`. Either way the route then re-runs with its retained snapshot or the bridge's newest. That is an
event, with no polling and no timer. Test (stand-ins): `window_pixels` returns None, then the edge fires, and the
retained snapshot is drawn with no new revision. The control is the current route, which draws nothing. Also run the
parity tool once unprimed, as a reported case rather than a gate.

### M4. A content failure in the core is invisible to the route: the card stays shown over a stale or never-written target

`spectrum.cpp:669-684` and `mode_content.cpp:883-895` (`keepsTarget`: latched, so the target "keeps what it holds"),
`surfaces.cpp:255-266` (the record fails: no pass), `native_route.py:303-310` (shown on the first publish whatever the
core did).

Round 2 says "the route fades the card out on failure". That is true of Python-side failures only. The core's
`ContentLatch` keeps the target, and nothing reports it to the route.

**Failure scenarios.**

- The first build fails: a driver refuses the pipeline, or a pack lacks the backend's variant. The surface's texture
  has never been written. On Vulkan its contents are undefined, possibly recycled memory. The composite draws it at
  once, for the whole activation.
- A program change whose build fails. The target keeps the old program's last frame. The card shows it frozen while
  the music plays.
- Latched, then a new activation with the same program, settings and card. `keepsTarget` returns true before any
  `hasFrame` check, so the previous activation's last frame shows at once (`fade_ms = 0`).

**Suggested fix.**

- When a content fails or is latched and the target holds nothing of its own (`m_drawnTexture` is null, or the size
  differs), have the surface clear the target once to transparent with its own pipeline.
- Expose a cheap failure edge the route reads at its sync: a counter accessor, not `state()` JSON. The route then
  treats a new failure like a frame failure: fade out, and fade in when the core draws again.
- Add a `generic_mode_check` case: no program, then a program whose build fails. Check that the target reads
  transparent and that the edge moved.

### M5. A persistent refused frame freezes the card indefinitely, with no fade and no log

`stream_source.h:24-26`, `spectrum.cpp:676-682`, `mode_content.cpp:888-893`. The refusal is counted in the state,
which nothing in the product reads.

**Failure scenario.** A simulation that keeps producing a non-finite value, for example a normalisation dividing by a
silent zero. Every frame is refused, and `keepsTarget` keeps the last good frame. The Visualizer stands frozen while
the music plays, for as long as the refusals last, with nothing logged. R1 M4 asked to keep the target instead of
clearing it. That is right for one frame, but it is unbounded here.

**Suggested fix.** Bound the keep. A run of refusals longer than the content fade (or N frames) raises the M4 failure
edge, so the route fades the card out and fades it back in at the next good frame. Better at the source: refuse a
non-finite value in `NativeLayout.pack` or the writer, so the route's own frame-failure path (fade out, retry per
snapshot, logged once) handles it before the core. Test: a stream of NaN records past the bound gives the edge and a
fade. The control is the current freeze.

### M6. `v_uv` from `mode.vert` is the target's, in the backend's clip orientation, not the item's: product 2D shaders would misregister

`native/core/shaders/mode.vert:12` (`v_uv = position * 0.5 + 0.5` over the target),
`mode_content.cpp:249-250` (`u_quick_item_coords = 1` for every mode). Readers:

- `devcurve.frag:269-270`, `bubble.frag:127-128` and `oscilloscope.frag:355-356` all take `fc = v_uv * u_resolution`
  under `u_quick_item_coords == 1`. They have no `u_item_from_frag` rows: only Spectrum reads those.

The target and the item differ:

- The target is the composite rect: the card's outer rect enlarged to whole, quad-aligned device pixels
  (`rendering/native/spectrum.py:104-105`, `geometry.rect`).
- `u_resolution` is the item's oriented logical size.

**Failure scenario.** An N5 port of Spline Curve, Oscilloscope or Bubble on the generic content:

- At a fractional ratio or an odd origin, every coordinate is shifted by the target's outward snapping (up to two
  device pixels per edge) and scaled by target over item. The border, the content rect and the curves are off by one
  to two device pixels against the Python renderer, so rule (a) cannot be bit-identical.
- On OpenGL and D3D (clip y up) the rows are flipped. The product draws natively on Vulkan only, but the harness
  compares Vulkan with OpenGL.

Probe Rings cannot see any of this:

- its glow pass is radially symmetric and centred on the target;
- the draw round runs at origin 0, quarters 0 and exact sizes (`generic_mode_check.py:73-74`, `:461`).

That is why "the generic pass takes the product 2D shaders' shape" passed.

**Suggested fix.** Give `mode.vert` a small core block: the affine map from target uv to the item's oriented logical
uv, composed in C++ from the card (`item_from_window` · `window_from_target`), with the clip-space row correction from
`QRhi::isYUpInNDC()`. `v_uv` is then the Python quad's on every backend. It is affine, and one triangle interpolates it
exactly. Add a harness case: an asymmetric `v_uv` pass (a corner marker or a gradient) at dpr 1.5, an odd card origin
and four rotations, on both APIs, against the Python renderer's quad or the computed expectation.

## Low

- **L1. The GUI-thread swapchain query runs at every route's first sync** (`composite.cpp:47-68`, `:630-639`).
  - Each route makes its composite in `_window_metrics` and reads `window_pixels()` at once, before the composite has
    synced. So the GUI-thread `rendererInterface()->getResource(RhiSwapchainResource)` and `currentPixelSize()` run on
    the ordinary path, not only at startup.
  - Qt documents the renderer interface's resource queries as render-thread functionality. This is an unsynchronised
    read of the swapchain pointer and size while the render thread may be creating or resizing it.
  - Fix: no GUI-thread read. The value is invalid until the composite's first sync, which M3's edge then reports.
- **L2. A JSON snapshot of the reader per accepted frame, on the render thread** (`stream_source.cpp:183-187`, built by
  `packet_reader.cpp:762-810`).
  - About forty inserts, nested objects and arrays, every frame while playing, for diagnostics only. In round 1 it was
    built on the GUI thread on demand. The fix for R1 L9 made it per frame.
  - This is against "diagnostic instrumentation adds no recurring per-frame work".
  - Fix: copy a POD struct of counters under `m_reportMutex`, and build the JSON in `stateJson()` on the GUI thread.
- **L3. The unbind fade fades an already-cleared target, and a raising `spectrum.unbind()` skips the hide**
  (`native_route.py:146-149`; `stream_source.cpp:188-191` leads to the clear at `spectrum.cpp:811-830`).
  - The stream retires first, so the next sync clears the card. The 1.3 s fade then runs over transparent texels,
    which is a vanish. This is not a regression: round 1 hid the item at once.
  - The fade's `composite.set` sits after `spectrum.unbind()` in the same `try`. If the unbind raises, the card stays
    shown with its last frame (probe C confirmed the order).
  - Fix: retire the stream at the fade's end (the composite's `finished`), or let the content keep its target while
    its item fades out after an unbind. Guard the hide on its own.
- **L4. The content latch re-arms on every step of a fade, and its keys are raw pointers** (`spectrum.cpp:580-591`,
  `mode_content.cpp:748-755`, `:886`, `:904`).
  - `drawnInputs` and `drawnGeneration` move with every card change, `content_fade` included. A failing build is
    therefore retried every frame of a 1.3 s fade, or of an expanded transition, although no pipeline depends on the
    card.
  - The keys `rhi` and `pass` can repeat after a target is released and recreated at the same address.
  - Fix: re-arm on the program, settings, rhi and a target generation counter (not its address), plus a bounded
    retry.
- **L5. `FAILURE_FADE_MS` is a second owner of the owner's private constant** (`native_route.py:34-36`, held equal by
  `tests/test_native_visualizer_route.py:450-454`).
  - Import `_ACTIVATION_SCENE_FADE_DURATION_S` (or a public alias) instead.
  - Separately, after a presentation-`None` hide (`_hide` sets `_faded_out`), the reappearance fades twice: the
    composite's 1.3 s fade times any scene or content fade the owner applies.
- **L6. The core members' values are a third list that zero-fills silently** (`mode_content.cpp:231-268`).
  - `modeCoreValues` is an if-chain that zeroes every name it does not handle and returns true.
  - A row added to `kModeCoreMembers` and `MODE_CORE_MEMBERS` without its branch uploads zeros. Both drift tests
    pass. Only the harness's `core_values` (a KeyError) would notice.
  - Fix: each table row carries its value function, and a test checks that every row's value moves with its card key.
- **L7. Test gaps.** No test pins presets, preset values or UI wording.
  - `test_the_routes_teardown_branches_never_raise...` (`test_native_visualizer_route.py:414`):
    - it has no negative control;
    - it never checks the mapping was closed and unlinked, which is L2's promise;
    - its comment speaks of "a mapping whose close raises" but injects a raising `spectrum.unbind`.
  - `test_the_routes_fade_is_the_visualizers_activation_fade` mirrors two constants (L5).
  - Product shader text is pinned:
    - Bubble's sampler names, at `test_native_mode_content.py:274` and `generic_mode_check.py:295`;
    - the C++ declaration `class SpectrumSource final : public StreamSource`, at `test_native_core_spectrum.py:233`.

    A rename breaks them without a behaviour change.
  - `card_members` (`test_native_mode_content.py:283`) scans renderer source with a regex. It fails open on any other
    spelling and skips `oscilloscope.py`.
  - `generic_mode_check`'s fade check reads the `shown` and `fading` flags at the start and the end only. It has no
    pixels during the fade and no fade-in on recovery.
  - Nothing covers M1 to M6.
- **L8. `against_baseline` falls back to aggregate keys when either report lacks frame hashes**
  (`owner_render_parity.py:744-769`).
  - In that case equal difference rows (input, count, max level, first 200 rows) are reported as "same". That does not
    prove bit-identical native frames.
  - The round's "hash-identical to N2.5's own commit" holds only if N2.5's baseline was re-run with hashes. The
    commit's own report predates `native_hashes`.
  - Fix: report the fallback as "difference-identical", never "same", or refuse it for an identity verdict.

## What was not verified

- No GPU run. M4 (undefined target contents on Vulkan), M5 and M6 are read from the code and the shaders; M1 and M2
  are shown by the probe above.
- Whether the product ever activates a Visualizer before its window's first expose (M3 case 1) was not traced through
  DisplayManager. Whether the engine publishes revisions at rest was not traced either.
- Qt's documentation on the renderer interface's thread rules (L1) and the swapchain resize order (before or after
  sync, within a frame) are from Qt 6 as I know it, not read in the pinned 6.12 source.
- The `--against` run that made rule (a) "hash-identical to N2.5's own commit" was not re-run (L8).
