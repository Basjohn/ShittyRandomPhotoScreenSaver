# Steam Games You Follow | Product and acceptance contract

**Active owner:** `Current_Plan.md`. This is a product reference and unresolved acceptance specification, not a checkpoint diary. The existing `steam_progress` persisted identity is the canonical Games You Follow widget. It starts disabled. All associated options remain within canonical `widgets.steam_progress` and shared Steam settings.

## Source truth and refresh

- The credential owner supplies the linked Steam identity; the followed-set endpoint supplies the actual followed AppIDs. Never substitute owned, recently played, wishlist or a guessed selection. The public `ISteamNews/GetNewsForApp/v2` endpoint is **per game** and does not expose Steam's separately personalized What's New ranking; no source may claim exact personalized-feed parity.
- The card ranks **all discovered, validated English/unknown-language stories globally by descending published UTC timestamp**, not by follow-list order, round-robin position, or per-game slot. Keep only the latest eight distinct article identities, retaining prior winners across bounded batches and replacing winners when their app is rechecked. Deduplicate app/GID, normalized equivalent headlines and identical-second same-app translations. Reject explicit non-English language codes, known foreign-language title labels and non-Latin title scripts. When Steam provides no language metadata, an unknown Latin-script title is only provisionally English: it is impossible to guarantee language or all translation-family identities from this documented endpoint without an additional verified source. Never claim otherwise.
- An initial session validates the followed set; each bounded source job fetches **at most four app-news feeds**, each with at most eight candidate rows. The active generation-shared owner advances coverage on its **one existing cancellable single-shot deadline** while a real consumer is active. An initial incomplete sweep advances after 15 seconds following each completed batch. For 142 follows, complete coverage takes 36 jobs: 35 four-app jobs and a final two-app job, with no duplicate wrap inside that initial sweep. **Full initial coverage is durable**, not reset on the next refresh. Once it is complete, the regular shared Steam refresh interval admits one maintenance session of up to eight games, as two existing four-feed jobs separated by 15 seconds; after the second job it returns to the normal interval. A persisted next-app cursor advances across sessions and restarts, with oldest-checked apps eventually revisited without a rapid 142-game repeat. At a six-minute interval, a 142-game set is revisited across roughly 18 maintenance sessions (about 108 minutes while the card stays active), not every six minutes. No new scheduler is introduced. The initial view is explicitly marked `Checking X / Y followed games`; a complete ranked result cannot be promised before full coverage. This active sweep entails significant Steam API traffic; Windows network, rate-limit and responsiveness acceptance is required, not inferred from source-only tests.
- One source/lease per active runtime generation across displays. There is no QML timer, per-card provider, rescheduling on resize, repeated image decoding during gestures, or new Settings authority. Manual refresh enters this same shared source and supersedes its pending deadline. On final lease retirement, cancel the deadline, retire/fence the source and reject queued results. Respect backend rate-limit/backoff; errors must never immediately arm a fast retry.
- Persist the profile-private followed-set membership for an in-progress sweep and a bounded, durable per-app candidate map (up to eight stories per app, up to 512 followed games), alongside the ranked last-good top eight. With ~142 followed games, a restart retains the discovered runner-up stories, so replacing a game's obsolete news can promote another game's next newest story rather than re-probing everything from scratch. An in-progress initial sweep resumes its saved follow list. After complete coverage, the validated followed list is reused for at most 24 hours; the next maintenance refresh revalidates membership after that interval. A changed follow set rebuilds coverage only when membership actually changes, dropping unfollowed games from the candidate map. An empty followed set is rechecked on the regular interval. These cached candidates are not a live feed: newer news requires a later bounded source refresh. A recently completed cache is displayed immediately on startup; the same existing shared owner waits for the remainder of the configured interval before a small maintenance session, or responds to explicit manual refresh. If the process exits between the two four-game jobs, the pending second slice resumes through that same owner and its single deadline after reactivation. A failure retains the last-good cursor and ranked results and returns to the ordinary interval/backoff rather than spinning. Larger followed sets retain the older top-eight fallback rather than an unbounded private store. Do not cache raw provider URL, credential, SteamID or HTML; do not refresh the last-good timestamp on error. Distinguish no follows, no news after observed coverage, pending sweep, stale cache and unavailable. Show `Checking X / Y followed games` while building coverage, but do not label an otherwise normal static fallback `CACHED UPDATES`. The UI must not present a partial four-game sample as the latest news across all follows.

## Action, imagery and text

- The validated app-news response binds a positive requested AppID and bounded decimal news GID. Clicks carry **only a currently visible slot** into Python. Re-resolve the current private identity, construct an exact canonical Store news-view target, and independently validate it at the display manager before the existing secure Steam action route. Never hand QML provider URLs, private AppIDs/GIDs or arbitrary HTML. The provider's external/community `url` is neither displayed nor launched; it must not make an otherwise valid Steam news identity inert. Do not claim live links work until physically clicked on Windows.
- The followed AppID, not article headline text or ownership, determines the **game label on every row**. A single profile-private, durable `games_followed_app_metadata` cache retains verified AppID → canonical game name and any accepted Store artwork URL. Seed it from existing owned-game/last-good caches; resolve unknown followed AppIDs through at most four worker-only, single-AppID Store metadata lookups per admitted source refresh (no complete app-catalogue download). A verified name survives restart and Store outages indefinitely. Unresolved names display `Steam App <AppID>` only during first hydration or genuine failure; never persist that fallback as a verified name. Cache-only startup and Settings do not contact the Store; failed lookups have a six-hour negative TTL.
- Use the existing Steam asset cache and worker to validate locally decoded artwork, including the Store header source when available; a corrupt or missing image never invalidates its article or hides another game's available artwork. Per-game artwork fetches are bounded to four AppIDs per ordinary source refresh; failed artwork has a one-day retry floor. A complete accepted source revision publishes once into the eight retained article slots; it does not republish per individual image download. If any retained story has artwork, all visible tiles share the same image rail and tiles missing images display a stable local placeholder; if none has artwork, all tiles use the same text-only arrangement. The display uses only validated local image URIs, and invisible retained tiles release their image source. No article-cover scraper, QML remote images, second image worker or new refresh timer.
- Headline, published date, feed label and bounded plain-text preview remain legible. Remove HTML, BBCode and Steam image macros. Prefer responsive font shrink/wrap before elision, honoring the user's separate headline length setting. In an extremely small card, hide whole story sections rather than overlap the header or footer.

## Retained QML, chrome and CUSTOM

- Use the ordinary retained Qt Quick host, its canonical card border width, outer directional shadow, text shadows, semantic palette and shared `BrandedHeader`. The refresh frame is optional and defaults off. Inner tiles may use only bounded retained shadows consistent with peers. Preserve the shared outer card shadow owner. No bespoke header primitive or competing theme/border setting.
- Expose **four stable, persisted roles**: `header`, `refresh`, `story_tiles` (one grouped role, no per-story persistent handles), `overflow_summary`. The shared selected editor owns gesture handling, independent child X/Y geometry, flip, Save/Cancel/Undo/Restore, slots and persistence. Family QML owns authored reflow and content placement only; never change the core editor to compensate for a card-local defect.
- The header flip changes **placement, logo/text order and appropriate content flow**, never pixel-mirrors art or text. Live unsaved flip survives further outer/child resizing; flipped free X offsets remain editable. Independent story-group width/height scales affect the actual selected target dimensions. A sibling/footer may limit **paint capacity**, not clamp the editable target's Y dimension so that the user's handle stops working. Only complete tiles paint inside the declared card rail. Image/text room determines columns before images are hidden; an image-capable ~1,570px wide rail wraps five readable tiles into two rows rather than eight narrow no-image slivers.
- Available story tiles and the refresh control give a faint retained hover outline and pointing-hand cue like Achievement Pulse artwork; the outline is painted above the tile's image/text and only when the existing action can run. No hover timer, independent animation, network request or render loop. Hover and article activation are disabled under shared CUSTOM input capture. Refresh vanishes before intersecting the painted header or leaving the card's bounds at extreme portrait size, and reappears when room returns. Retain all eight delegates on resize and suppress their image sources when hidden. Parent preferred size must not depend on child-edit geometry, visible row count or provider data; moving/resizing a child may not perform source or Settings I/O.
