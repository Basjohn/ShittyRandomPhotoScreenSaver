## 2. What the newest real screenshot *does* establish

- A continuous family of luminous woven horizontal/curving strips with clear orange/teal separation and fine internal lattice/strand lines is already achievable in the live OpenGL/Quick renderer. Preserve useful surface readability while improving shape, music and grading.
- The basic non-idle **alive** undulation is fairly good. It should not be rewritten merely because the older plan called all shape responses failures. However, strong musically triggered motion still reads as too much twitch/horizontal elongation, not the dramatic smooth *vertical* and rotational gestures in the references.
- The dark wet floor supports the scene sufficiently to be a provisional baseline; its chief immediate objection is **repetitive vertical reflection streaks/columns**. Foreground and distance should maintain similar neutral wet concrete character, not bloom into patterned blue patches, glossy mirrored stripes, puddles or new textures.
- The mist/atmosphere direction is now **liked** with local waveform-tinted scattering; not an invitation to re-create an older dense-bank mock, blanket black scene, fog cuboid, floating cloud or washed-out glow. Improvements must be restrained and actually needed.
- The screenshot alone does **not** show reactive time domain, real audio-to-pixel causality, idle speed, color shift under music, floor collisions, hidden history ends or source/output cost. Do not infer acceptance of any of these.

## 4. Floor and mist budget: explicit stop-loss

- **Floor is provisionally accepted once the streak/column artefact is softened.** Preserve the current surface and its overall light level. Only return for definite contact/viewport correctness, a new operator request, or after Waveform and Studio are genuinely accepted and there is spare time/benefit.
- A possible implementation is reduced anisotropic streak emphasis plus spatially broader/fuzzier aggregated reflection **without losing hue and broad luminance**, but this is *not* a mandate for a new reflection renderer, gaussian shader or expensive blur. Inspect actual current implementation first. One controlled A/B is preferable to a week of speculative material tweaks.
- Mist is **a good direction**, not a promise of perfection: protect existing light tint, night density, softness and no obvious geometric mass while evaluating local clearance and actual reflection/volume performance. Reject improvements if they destroy the current positive mood.
- Colour and reactivity are much higher value than floor polish. Studio is strategically important but should bind after motion semantics stabilize, while shell/visual design can be prototyped independently.

## 5. Compare the *music*, not the picture alone

For each meaningful visual change, compare fixed pose/viewport across digital idle, quietly playing, ordinary, strong sustained passage and transient peak with unchanged real recorded PCM, source identity and effective preset. Provide an uninterrupted 10s normal-speed recording and a 5-frame labelled visual sequence. For peak/quiet images, verify underlying same world/camera/identities and meaningful musical input; do not fake peaks by switching presets or waving synthetic amplitude after publication. The real GPU renderer, source path and conditional glow/particles must create the difference.

**Never claim the mock's exact high-fidelity 2D rendered highlights are inherently attainable at a given cost or that an image overlay is a valid native performance benchmark.** Prefer faithful organic motion, physical continuity and readable colour contrast over blindly reproducing a single extravagant still.
