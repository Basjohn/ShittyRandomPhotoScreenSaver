# Games You Follow | Current product and source contract

**Owner:** existing `steam_progress` ordinary widget, `core/steam/games_followed_source.py`, `widgets/steam_followed_runtime.py`, and the retained Qt Quick family. This is the current product contract, not an implementation plan or a claim of physical acceptance. The existing `steam_progress` persisted identity is the canonical Games You Follow widget. It starts disabled. All associated options remain within canonical `widgets.steam_progress` and shared Steam settings.

## Source truth and refresh

- The credential owner supplies the linked Steam identity; the followed-set endpoint supplies the actual followed AppIDs. Never substitute owned, recently played, wishlist or a guessed selection. The public `ISteamNews/GetNewsForApp/v2` endpoint is **per game** and does not expose Steam's separately personalized What's New ranking; no source may claim exact personalized-feed parity.
- The card ranks **all discovered, validated English/unknown-language stories globally by descending published UTC timestamp**, not by follow-list order, round-robin position, or per-game slot. Keep only the latest eight distinct article identities, retaining prior winners across bounded batches and replacing winners when their app is rechecked. Deduplicate app/GID, normalized equivalent headlines and identical-second same-app translations. Reject explicit non-English language codes, known foreign-language title labels and non-Latin title scripts. When Steam provides no language metadata, an unknown Latin-script title is only provisionally English: it is impossible to guarantee language or all translation-family identities from this documented endpoint without an additional verified source. Never claim otherwise.
- An initial session validates the followed set; each bounded source job fetches **at most four app-news feeds**, each with at most eight candidate rows. The active generation-shared owner advances coverage on its **one existing cancellable single-shot deadline** while a real consumer is active. An initial incomplete sweep advances after 15 seconds following each completed batch. Complete coverage processes each followed app once in four-feed jobs, with no duplicate wrap inside the initial sweep. **Full initial coverage is durable**, not reset on the next refresh. Once it is complete, the regular shared Steam refresh interval admits one maintenance session of up to eight games, as two existing four-feed jobs separated by 15 seconds; after the second job it returns to the normal interval. A persisted next-app cursor advances across sessions and restarts, with oldest-checked apps eventually revisited without immediately repeating the entire followed set. The default six-minute interval spreads maintenance across successive eight-app sessions rather than repeating an entire completed follow-list sweep every interval. No new scheduler is introduced. The initial view is explicitly marked `Checking X / Y followed games`; a complete ranked result cannot be promised before full coverage. This active sweep entails bounded but real Steam API traffic. Changes to its request cadence must be evaluated for network, rate-limit and responsiveness impact; source-only tests do not establish measured cost.
- One source/lease per active runtime generation across displays. There is no QML timer, per-card provider, rescheduling on resize, repeated image decoding during gestures, or new Settings authority. Manual refresh enters this same shared source and supersedes its pending deadline. On final lease retirement, cancel the deadline, retire/fence the source and reject queued results. Respect backend rate-limit/backoff; errors must never immediately arm a fast retry.
- Persist the profile-private followed-set membership for an in-progress sweep and a bounded, durable per-app candidate map (up to eight stories per app, up to 512 followed games), alongside the ranked last-good top eight. A restart retains discovered runner-up stories, so replacing one app's obsolete news can promote another app's next newest story without an immediate full sweep. An in-progress initial sweep resumes its saved follow list. After complete coverage, the validated followed list is reused for at most 24 hours; the next maintenance refresh revalidates membership after that interval. A changed follow set rebuilds coverage only when membership actually changes, dropping unfollowed games from the candidate map. An empty followed set is rechecked on the regular interval. These cached candidates are not a live feed: newer news requires a later bounded source refresh. A recently completed cache is displayed immediately on startup; the same existing shared owner waits for the remainder of the configured interval before a small maintenance session, or responds to explicit manual refresh. If the process exits between the two four-game jobs, the pending second slice resumes through that same owner and its single deadline after reactivation. A failure retains the last-good cursor and ranked results and returns to the ordinary interval/backoff rather than spinning. Larger followed sets retain the older top-eight fallback rather than an unbounded private store. Cache only a validated, AppID-associated Steam article URL, never an unvalidated provider/external URL, credential, SteamID or HTML; do not refresh the last-good timestamp on error. Distinguish no follows, no news after observed coverage, pending sweep, stale cache and unavailable. Show `Checking X / Y followed games` while building coverage, but do not label an otherwise normal static fallback `CACHED UPDATES`. The UI must not present a partial four-game sample as the latest news across all follows.

## Action, imagery and text

- The app-news response binds a validated requested AppID and decimal news GID, but that GID is **not an article-view URL ID**. Admit the source-provided `url` only when it is an exact HTTPS Steam article route: `store.steampowered.com/news/app/<same AppID>/view/<event ID>`, `steamcommunity.com/app/<same AppID>/announcements/detail/<event ID>`, or a Steam Community `/games/<safe vanity slug>/announcements/detail/<event ID>` delivered by the validated AppID-specific Steam news response. Do not equate the event ID and the API GID. Also admit Steam's source-provided syndicated `https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/<feed>/<same news GID>` redirect (and its exact Steam Store-hosted equivalent); this Steam-owned link leads to the publisher's article rather than the game news index. Validate the exact host, narrowly bounded feed segment, matching decimal GID, and canonical URL encoding; never accept an arbitrary publisher URL or caller-supplied redirect destination. Reject other domains, alternate paths, encoded destinations, queries, fragments, ports, userinfo, invalid AppID and invalid GID without losing the story's text or images. Store only the validated URL inside the profile-private ranked/per-game cache. QML receives only current visible ordinal slots, never the URL or AppID/GID. The private model resolves the accepted slot and revalidates the link before invoking the display manager; the display manager independently validates the restricted Steam article route again before handing the URL to the existing secure Steam launcher. Every accepted story remains clickable: the private current-slot action first attempts its validated article URL, then falls back explicitly to `https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/<AppID>/`, the bounded game news **index**, if that exact article URL is absent or unsupported. This includes older last-good caches whose former action flag was false. A fallback is not represented as the specific article. Already persisted stories whose source URL was discarded by older checkpoints cannot recover that URL from the stored GID alone. They retain the hub fallback until the app's regular bounded news refresh observes the source-provided link; avoid constructing a guessed URL or forcing a whole-list refresh. The display manager independently validates the exact hub route and app number before dispatch, and the existing helper still owns opening and screensaver-exit consequences. A malformed cached article URL cannot be opened, but also must not erase the otherwise valid story. Never invent a `/view/<GID>` URL, launch arbitrary provider URLs or perform a network request just for a click. Steam's vetted `/externalpost/` is a source-supplied redirect: its eventual publisher page is outside the project's control. A Steam-provided URL may still fail on Steam's website; source validity does not prove actual browser load. Real Windows click and page-content acceptance is separate from source tests.
- The followed AppID, not article headline text or ownership, determines the **game label on every row**. A single profile-private, durable `games_followed_app_metadata` cache retains verified AppID → canonical game name and any accepted Store artwork URL. Seed it from existing owned-game/last-good caches; resolve unknown followed AppIDs through at most four worker-only, single-AppID Store metadata lookups per admitted source refresh (no complete app-catalogue download). A verified name survives restart and Store outages indefinitely. Unresolved names display `Steam App <AppID>` only during first hydration or genuine failure; never persist that fallback as a verified name. Cache-only startup and Settings do not contact the Store; failed lookups have a six-hour negative TTL.
- Use the existing Steam asset cache and worker to validate locally decoded artwork, including the Store header source when available; a corrupt or missing image never invalidates its article or hides another game's available artwork. Per-game artwork fetches are bounded to four AppIDs per ordinary source refresh; failed artwork has a one-day retry floor. A complete accepted source revision publishes once into the eight retained article slots; it does not republish per individual image download. If any retained story has artwork, all visible tiles share the same image rail and tiles missing images display a stable local placeholder; if none has artwork, all tiles use the same text-only arrangement. The display uses only validated local image URIs, and invisible retained tiles release their image source. No article-cover scraper, QML remote images, second image worker or new refresh timer.
- Headline, published date, feed label and bounded plain-text preview remain legible. Strip HTML, BBCode, full Steam Clan image macros and standalone clan-image paths from the preview; raw image addresses are never prose. Keep at most three validated relative Clan CDN image references per story in the same durable article record. Previously persisted story rows can contain valid relative image paths inside their plain-text preview but no image-reference field: normalize the **ranked snapshot and per-game candidate buckets** together on worker/cache read, recover only validated Clan-image references from that legacy preview, and remove the image paths from prose. Retain source identity, title, game label, ranking and click target, and let the next ordinary source commit persist normalized candidates; neither a forced full-followed-list refresh nor a separate migration/cache owner is justified. On the existing source-worker refresh, reuse valid local thumbnail files or make at most three fresh image requests for the *whole source refresh*, with a persisted one-day failure floor. A source revision projects only existing local file URIs; retain image/body association across ranked article swaps, cache-only startup and retirement. Emit at most one source-worker summary per decorated result containing validated reference count, locally admitted image count, network attempts and cache-only status when image refs exist; never log raw image addresses, issue per-card traces or add polling. The thumbnail rail occupies the bounded preview space when image and text dimensions permit, without displacing game name, headline, date or article action. Its **actual painted** image count is derived from the retained tile's current X/Y geometry. With two or three painted images, suppress the body-text preview so it cannot be cramped alongside a multi-image rail; with one or zero painted images, show the text when the tile has a readable preview lane. Resizing or CUSTOM adjustments can change this decision locally without source work or rebuilding the retained model. An absent/failed thumbnail leaves meaningful text intact and cannot suppress unrelated game artwork. Prefer responsive font shrink/wrap before elision, honoring the separate headline limit. In an extremely small card, hide whole story sections rather than overlap header/footer.

## Retained QML, chrome and CUSTOM

- Use the ordinary retained Qt Quick host, its canonical outer-card border width, outer directional shadow, text shadows, semantic palette and shared `BrandedHeader`. The refresh frame is optional and defaults off. Story-tile base borders add 0.25 logical px to the family's previous base stroke and hover stroke; this is a local optical adjustment, not a new global border setting. Draw thin `headerBorderColor` semantic outlines above every game-identity image and every article-body thumbnail so image pixels cannot obscure borders. Inner tiles may use only bounded retained shadows consistent with peers. Preserve the shared outer card shadow owner. No bespoke header primitive or competing theme/border setting.
- Expose **four stable, persisted roles**: `header`, `refresh`, `story_tiles` (one grouped role, no per-story persistent handles), `overflow_summary`. The shared selected editor owns gesture handling, independent child X/Y geometry, flip, Save/Cancel/Undo/Restore, slots and persistence. Family QML owns authored reflow and content placement only; never change the core editor to compensate for a card-local defect.
- The header flip changes **placement, logo/text order and appropriate content flow**, never pixel-mirrors art or text. Live unsaved flip survives further outer/child resizing; flipped free X offsets remain editable. Independent story-group width/height scales affect the actual selected target dimensions. A sibling/footer may limit **paint capacity**, not clamp the editable target's Y dimension so that the user's handle stops working. Only complete tiles paint inside the declared card rail. Image/text room determines columns before images are hidden; a wide image-capable rail wraps readable tiles into additional rows rather than forcing narrow image-free columns.
- Available story tiles and the refresh control give a faint retained hover outline and pointing-hand cue like Achievement Pulse artwork; the outline is painted above the tile's image/text and only when the existing action can run. No hover timer, independent animation, network request or render loop. Hover and article activation are disabled under shared CUSTOM input capture. Refresh vanishes before intersecting the painted header or leaving the card's bounds at extreme portrait size, and reappears when room returns. Retain all eight delegates on resize and suppress their image sources when hidden. Parent preferred size must not depend on child-edit geometry, visible row count or provider data; moving/resizing a child may not perform source or Settings I/O.

## Disk and network resource budget

The ordinary per-profile Steam `assets` directory contains managed fingerprint-named cached game art, achievement icons, avatars and transforms. Prune its *managed images* after successful cache writes by oldest modification time when either 256 files or 384 MiB is exceeded; exempt the newly written asset and never prune temporary/non-managed files. A successful cache hit, repaint, resize, cache-only read or disabled widget never starts maintenance. An image under active presentation can remain open and should be revalidated on a later generation if physically removed; retention of *all* independently retained Steam consumer artwork has not been proven by this worker-local cap.

The separate `assets/news_inline` subtree is restricted to 96 images or 64 MiB per profile, oldest-first on a successful new inline download, protecting images associated with the currently accepted source snapshot; it does not compete with the game-art budget. Thumbnail downloads have a three-attempt per-source-refresh ceiling and a five-second timeout each. A profile cache can temporarily exceed its limit before the next successful write or when protected images alone exceed the budget. Limits are per profile and per category: neither this contract nor the FEEDS 8 MiB-per-record guard proves an aggregate, installation-wide quota across Steam profiles, wallpaper, FEEDS and other cache families. An app-wide quota/inventory requires a separate ownership/retention audit, not an unreviewed global delete pass.

## Change and regression contract

A source or QML change must retain bounded cache-first admission, timestamp ranking, followed-set provenance, shared deadline retirement, stable delegates, semantic header/theme parity and actual painted CUSTOM X/Y/flip geometry. Recheck default QML visible story count against the Python authored layout across wide, tall and grid shapes; customized child rails may calculate their own bounded paint capacity but may not rewrite the parent preferred extent. Use native Qt for paint/interaction claims and a bounded physical check for affected visual or source behavior. Report test evidence against the exact changed tree; automated coverage does not certify unobserved appearance or real network parity.
